# Review 2 — ethnographic framing, 2026-09-16

**Core suggestion:** frame the paper as a *computational ethnographic case study* — "ethnography" without qualification invites reviewer objections (participant observation, interviews, reflexivity); "computational ethnographic case study" is precise and safer.

**Framing paragraph (adapted for our hybrid case):**

> We present a computational ethnographic case study of a long-running hybrid human–AI agent organization. Rather than treating the system as a controlled experiment, we reconstruct its evolving organizational practices from the durable traces it produced: commits, governance rules, evaluation procedures, task records, and other artifacts. Our goal is descriptive and mechanism-generating: to document how locally reasonable attempts at correction accumulated into an increasingly burdensome governance regime — a process we term a bureaucratization cascade. The evidence should be read as a detailed observational account of one organizational trajectory, not as a causal estimate of the effect of governance constraints on productivity. The value of the case lies in exposing a candidate mechanism, identifying measurable signatures, and motivating controlled replication.

**Key consequences:**
- Limitations become genre features: timeline, weird episodes, evaluator behavior, norm mutations are empirical material, not failed controls.
- Quantitative work (constraint counts, commit rates, enforcement changes) = **triangulation within the ethnography**, not the causal argument.
- Title option: *Bureaucratization Cascades in Autonomous Agent Organizations: A Computational Ethnography* — note our case is hybrid, not autonomous; adjust to "Hybrid Human–AI Organization."
