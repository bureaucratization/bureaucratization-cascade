# Review 1 — external reader, 2026-09-16

Verdict: strong case study, tighten rhetoric, make recovery causality quantitative.

Key points retained verbatim from the review discussion:

- **Protect at all costs:** the central ratchet story — incident → local hardening → no retirement mechanism → cumulative admission burden → throughput collapse. "That is the paper."
- **Name the phenomenon, don't prove a theory.** Reframe from "constraint accretion is the agent-era form of bureaucracy" to "we use recursive constraint accretion to describe this failure mode." Proposed definition:

  > Recursive constraint accretion: a process in which agents responding to local failures add persistent constraints faster than the organization retires or amortizes them, causing aggregate compliance burden to grow over time.

  Note: the definition deliberately does not require collapse (collapse is one possible outcome) and does not bake in "AI."
- **Highest-level claim** should become: "we document and name a failure pattern that, to our knowledge, has not been described in this form."
- **"Belongs on the list of multi-agent failure modes"** → "we propose it as a candidate multi-agent failure mode worth testing in other systems."
- **§4.3 (model comparison) is the shakiest section:** attributable window is Aug 8–Sep 16 while the collapse was earlier. Soften substantially, or relabel as "observations from the recovery-era window."
- **Recovery causality:** the Sept 13 config change coincides with a new model generation entering. Make causal evidence explicit: throughput/pass-rate immediately before/after the change, task mix held roughly constant. The "task pass-rate vs. cumulative gate count" figure is the missing piece.
- **Make the bankruptcy/rule-lifecycle asymmetry the conceptual center:** any self-modifying governance system needs rule lifecycle management, not just rule enforcement.
- **Editorial:** figure numbering in the PDF renders inconsistently (captions vs in-text references); trim colorful phrases ("eye of the needle," "bankruptcy mechanism," gpt-image-2 acknowledgment joke) for the arXiv version, preserve for the blog version.
